# Honeybee Biology and Pollination (fact sheet)

The western honeybee, Apis mellifera, lives in colonies of 20,000 to 80,000 individuals in temperate regions. A queen bee can lay up to 2,000 eggs per day during the spring peak, which exceeds her own body weight in eggs daily. Worker bees live about 6 weeks during the active summer season, while winter workers may survive for several months.

Honeybees communicate food locations through the waggle dance, described by Karl von Frisch, who received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1973 for this work. The angle of the waggle run relative to vertical on the comb encodes the direction of the food source relative to the sun, and the duration of the waggle phase encodes distance, with each additional 75 milliseconds corresponding to roughly one more kilometer of flight.

Honey production requires substantial foraging effort: a colony may visit around 100,000 flowers to produce one kilogram of honey, and a single forager carries about 40 to 70 milligrams of nectar per trip. The global production of natural honey was approximately 1.9 million tonnes in 2022, with China producing around 450,000 tonnes, the largest share of any country.

Colony collapse disorder drew global attention from 2006 onward, when US beekeepers began reporting sudden losses of 30 to 90 percent of hives in affected apiaries. Current science attributes most overwintering losses to a combination of the parasitic mite Varroa destructor, viral pathogens, pesticide exposure, and nutritional stress, rather than a single cause. Managed colony numbers in the United States have actually grown over the past decade despite high annual loss rates, because beekeepers rebuild colonies by splitting healthy ones.

About 75 percent of leading global food crop types benefit from animal pollination, and honeybees are the most widely managed pollinator species. Almond orchards in California alone require roughly 1.8 million colonies each February, which is more than two-thirds of all US managed hives, transported on trucks in what is the largest annual migration of pollinators on Earth.
